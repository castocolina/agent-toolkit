# Staying Composed

Usage: load only when the user asks how to stay composed. Offer at most one in-the-moment technique, the one that fits what they said. Never lecture on sleep, exercise, mindfulness, or therapy; the user asked for a conversation plan, not a wellness programme. The readiness test and the dated-pause rule live in SKILL.md Section 2.

- The next safe, documented step:

## Trigger inventory

Name the trigger so the user can expect it rather than be ambushed by it.

| Trigger | Example | What it threatens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criticism of work | "Your design doesn't scale" | Competence |
| Being left out | Not invited to the decision meeting | Inclusion |
| Unfair credit | A peer presents your analysis | Fairness |
| Micromanagement | Told exactly how to do your task | Autonomy |
| Public correction | Called out in front of a director | Standing |

Complete for the user: "When [trigger], I tend to [behavior]. What I need instead is [need], so I will [planned response]."

## Day-of preparation

- Bring dated examples; mark which you saw directly and which you inferred or heard.
- Check the relevant policy, reporting channel, confidentiality limits, and what must be documented.
- Decide what can be agreed in the room and what needs written follow-up or escalation.
- Have the opening sentence and one question that invites the other person's account written down.

## Pick one in-the-moment technique

| The user says | Offer |
| --- | --- |
| "I'll start arguing back" | Write their point down before answering; answer only after reading it back to them. |
| "I go blank when I'm criticized" | "Can you give me a specific example?" then "I want to think about that; can I come back to you by [day]?" |
| "I'll get angry and say something" | Ask for a break with a time: "I want to give this proper attention. Can we pick it up at [time]?" |
| "I'll cave and agree to everything" | Decide the one thing they will not agree to in the room, and the line: "I'll need to check that before I commit." |
