# Phrase Bank

Load when the user needs wording for a moment in the conversation: an opening for a topic SKILL.md Sections 3 to 5 do not cover, a way to show they heard without conceding, a line to buy time, a way to return to their point, a reframe of a blaming sentence, or a line for a defensive person or an impasse. Give one or two lines for the moment the user named, adapted to their facts; never the whole file. Behavior feedback, performance openers and feedback to a manager belong to feedback-mastery. Reactions to denial, deflection, attack and tears are in delivery-scripts.md; the closing lines are in closing-and-followup.md.

## Openings

An opening in the first two minutes has three parts: **purpose** ("I'd like to discuss [topic]"), **intent** ("My goal is to [understand / resolve / improve]") and an **invitation** ("I'd like to hear your perspective too"). Section 3 (a raise) puts the ask first, Section 4 (a decision) puts the decision first, and Section 5 (a conflict) names the behavior and its impact; use this formula when none of those fits.

Openers that start the conversation badly, because they sound like a verdict or an ambush: "We need to talk about your performance...", "I've noticed some problems...", "I've been meaning to tell you...".

| Tone | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Collaborative | "I'd like to talk about [topic]. My goal is to understand your perspective and share mine so we can [outcome]. Is this a good time?" |
| Direct | "We need to discuss [topic]. I want to be straightforward with you, and I also want to hear your side. Can we talk now?" |
| Curious | "I've noticed [observation] and I'd like to understand what's happening. Can you help me see your perspective?" |
| Clearing the air with a peer (relationship intact, pattern rather than one incident) | "I'd like to clear the air about [incident or pattern]. I value our working relationship and want to address this before it affects our collaboration. Can we talk?" |
| Short conflict opener | "I wanted to discuss what happened in yesterday's meeting. I'd like to understand your viewpoint and share mine so we can work together more effectively. Is now a good time?" |

The curious tone works only when the observation is an observable fact, not a judgment. If the facts are the dispute, use the Section 5 opening instead, which names the behavior and its impact. A raise opener is in Section 3, a sensitive personal matter in delivery-scripts.md.

## Showing you heard without conceding

- "I hear that you see it differently..."
- "I understand that's your experience..."
- "That makes sense from your perspective..."
- "I can see why you'd feel that way..."
- "Thank you for helping me understand your view..."

Follow one of these with your own point or question; alone it can sound like agreement.

## Redirecting to facts

When the conversation turns emotional or accusatory:

- "Let's go back to what actually happened..."
- "Can we focus on the specific incident?"
- "Setting aside intentions, what was the impact?"
- "What would a video camera have shown?"

## Buying time

Use these when you need a moment to think or to steady yourself. They buy seconds; if you need longer, ask for a break with a named time (SKILL.md Section 2: "come back to you by [day]").

- "That's an interesting point. Let me think about that."
- "I want to respond thoughtfully. Give me a moment."
- "I hadn't considered that angle. Let me process it."
- "Can you say more about that? I want to make sure I understand."
- "That's interesting. Tell me more about that."
- "I'm noticing a strong reaction in myself. Bear with me."
- "Can we slow down? I want to make sure I understand."

## Returning to your point

When the conversation has drifted or the other person deflected:

- "I want to make sure we address the main issue..."
- "Coming back to what we were discussing..."
- "Let me return to my original concern..."
- "Before we close, I want to revisit..."

If the drift is a counter-accusation, acknowledge it first, then ask to finish this point and come back to theirs (the Deflection row in delivery-scripts.md).

## Difficult moments

| Moment | Lines |
| --- | --- |
| They get defensive | "I can see this is bringing up strong feelings." "I'm not trying to attack you; I want to understand." "Let's slow down. What's your main concern right now?" |
| They deny or deflect | "Help me understand how you see it differently." "What would you need to see to agree there's an issue?" "Let's focus on what we can both agree happened." |
| You get triggered | "I need a moment to think about that." "I'm feeling defensive. Can you help me understand your point?" "Let's take a break and come back to this at [time]." |
| You hit an impasse | "We seem stuck. Can we agree on what we do agree on?" "What would help us move forward?" "Would it help to bring in [neutral party]?" |

A break after strong emotion needs twenty minutes or more, not five (the pause line in delivery-scripts.md, with its source). For a neutral party, the person must be someone both sides accept; for a dispute with an allegation or a reporting line between the parties, follow SKILL.md Sections 6 and 7.

## Reframes

Move a blaming sentence toward something the other person can answer. Offer the pair that matches the sentence the user plans to say.

| From | Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blame to curiosity | "Why did you miss the deadline?" | "Help me understand what happened with the timeline." |
| Blame to curiosity | "You should have told me." | "I would have liked to know earlier. What prevented that?" |
| Past to future | "You always do this." | "Going forward, how can we handle this differently?" |
| Past to future | "That presentation was a disaster." | "For the next presentation, let's talk about what would make it stronger." |
| Positions to interests | "I need you to do X." | "The outcome I'm trying to achieve is Y. What would work for you?" |
| Positions to interests | "That won't work for me." | "My concern with that is... What alternatives can we explore?" |

## When a conflict stays stuck

Four principles to return to: work from interests, not positions; acknowledge the emotions on both sides; look for options that work for both people; rebuild trust through small actions that are kept. Section 5 and Section 6 hold the procedure; these are the stance behind it.
