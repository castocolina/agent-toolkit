# Rehearsal Scripts

Load only to rehearse specific reactions, or to word a sensitive personal disclosure or an escalation. Openings, the negotiation objection table, and the summary format live in SKILL.md; do not restate them. Give the user one or two lines per reaction they expect, not the whole file.

## Reactions in a conflict

| Reaction | Sounds like | Line | Why this line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Denial | "That didn't happen." | "We remember it differently. The [ticket/thread] should show it; can we look after this? Either way, the impact was [impact]." | Moves the dispute to a record and keeps the impact on the table. |
| Deflection | "What about when you did X?" | "That's fair to raise, and I'll take it seriously. Can we finish this first and then come to it?" | Acknowledges without trading one issue for another. |
| Attack | "You're overreacting." | "Maybe. Tell me what you think I'm missing." | Refuses the fight without conceding the facts. |
| Shutdown | Silence, one-word answers | "I've done most of the talking. What's your view?" then wait at least ten seconds. | Filling the silence lets them stay out. |
| Tears or a raised voice | Visible distress | "Let's pause. Do you want ten minutes, or to pick this up tomorrow at [time]?" | A choice with a time keeps it a pause, not an end. |

## Reactions to a decision (SKILL.md Section 4)

| Reaction | Line | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bargaining: "Can't we try another quarter?" | "The decision is made. What I can influence is [timing/references/announcement]; let's use that." | Hinting it might change. |
| "Why me?" | The approved reason, once, then: "I know that's hard to hear." | New reasons, comparisons with colleagues. |
| "Did you agree with this?" | "It's the decision, and I'll help you with what comes next." | Distancing yourself from it; it invites appeals to you. |
| Silence or shock | "You don't need to respond now. Here are the next steps in writing; [contact] is available today." | Pushing for a reaction. |

## Raising a sensitive personal matter

> "I want to let you know about something that's affecting my work: [one-sentence version, e.g. a health issue]. I'm not going to go into detail. What would help is [specific adjustment], and I'll keep you updated on [what]."

Share the impact on work and the adjustment needed; diagnosis and private detail are optional, and usually belong with HR if formal accommodation is needed.

If the manager asks for detail the user does not want to share:

> "I'd rather keep the details private. What matters for work is [impact] and [adjustment]; if formal paperwork is needed, I'll go through HR."

The same shape works for family matters (a caring responsibility, a bereavement): impact on availability, the adjustment, and how long it is expected to last, if known.

## Escalating to a manager after trying directly

> "I need your help with something I've tried to resolve directly. What happened: [dated facts]. What I tried: [conversation on date, outcome]. Where it stands: [state]. I'm asking you to [specific help]."

Lead with what was tried; a manager's first question is usually "have you talked to them?"
