# Preparation Worksheet

Load only when the user asks for a written worksheet (the 10-minute quick prep or the full one), or when their goals, the time and place, or the way they are preparing needs work. Fill in what the user already gave; leave the rest as bracketed prompts, and keep only the add-on block for their route (SKILL.md Section 1).

## Quick prep (10 minutes)

For a user short on time: the worksheet below cut to what must be settled before speaking. Fill it with what they gave and ask only for what is missing.

```markdown
## Quick prep: [topic]

With / when: [name, planned date and time]

### Situation (facts only)
[What happened: observable behavior, dated, no judgment]

### Impact
[How it affected me, the team or the work]

### My goal
[The specific outcome I want]

### Their perspective
[How they might see it differently, in a sentence they would agree with]

### My opening line
[The first thing I will say]
```

## Full worksheet

```markdown
## Conversation prep: [topic]

With: [name, role, power relative to me]
When / where: [date, time, private place]; follow-up booked: [date]
Formal process involved? [none / HR / investigation / union]: check policy first if yes

### Facts (dated, checkable)
- [date]: [what happened], source: [ticket / thread / I saw it]

### Disputed
- [point]: settle with [record or person], or record as disputed

### My interpretations (keep out of the conversation)
- [assumed motive or intent]

### Their likely account, in one sentence they would agree with
[...]   (if I cannot write this, I am not ready; see SKILL.md Section 2)

### My part
[what I did or did not do that contributed]

### Outcome
Want: [specific change or decision]
Acceptable: [minimum]
Will not accept: [line], and what I do then: [fallback]

### Opening (written out)
[purpose + evidence/situation + ask or impact + open question]

### Reactions I expect and my one-line reply
- [reaction]: [reply]
```

## Add-on blocks

**Negotiation (compensation, promotion, workload):**

```markdown
- The ask, as a figure or title and a date: [...]
- Evidence since last adjustment: [launches, scope, review outcomes]
- Band / level criteria, if known: [...]; if unknown, ask for them
- Budget and cycle timing: when are calibration inputs fixed? [...]
- Tradable terms I would accept instead: [title / scope / bonus / review date / training]
- Who decides: [...]
- Market data for this role: [range, source, date]
- The company's financial situation, as far as it is known: [...]
```

**Delivering a decision:**

```markdown
- The decision in two sentences: [...]
- Approved reason (HR-agreed wording): [...]
- What they can still influence: [...]
- Written logistics to hand over: [dates, contacts, next steps]
- Support arranged if this is before a weekend or leave: [...]
```

**Conflict the user is in:**

```markdown
- Earlier attempts to raise it: [date, outcome]
- Others affected or present: [...]
- Is this a pattern or one event? [...]
- What we have in common that we can build on: [...]
- What resolution would look like for them: [...]
```

**Upward ask (a request to a manager):**

```markdown
- How the ask fits their priorities: [...]
- What is in it for them: [...]
- How it looks to their own stakeholders, and any politics around the decision: [...]
- Alternative I can propose if the first ask is rejected: [...]
- My best alternative if this fails (BATNA): [...]
```

## Goals

Settle three before the conversation:

1. **Outcome:** what specific change do I want?
2. **Relationship:** how do I want us to relate afterwards?
3. **Identity:** who do I want to be in this conversation (for example "calm and direct", "open and curious", "firm but fair")?

Reframe a goal the user states as a win over the other person:

| Ineffective goal | Effective goal |
| --- | --- |
| "Make them apologize" | "Understand what happened and agree on prevention" |
| "Prove I'm right" | "Share my perspective and understand theirs" |
| "Get them to change" | "Explore how we can work together differently" |

## Time and place

Defaults; adjust them to the people and the stakes:

- Not when either person is stressed, rushed or emotional, and not right before or after a high-stakes event.
- Allow at least 30 to 60 minutes; a facilitated session needs more (facilitation.md).
- A private, neutral room with no power imbalance (not the manager's office), away from distractions, with comfortable seating.
- If the slot falls before a weekend, leave or a long absence, keep it only with named support and a dated follow-up booked in the same conversation (SKILL.md Section 2).

## Extended worksheet block (optional)

The full worksheet is the default; append only the extended fields it lacks (for example emotional state, constraints, logistics) when the user wants them written out.

Add to the full worksheet when the user wants to process their feelings and the other person's view in writing.

```markdown
### My emotional state
Feeling: [angry / hurt / frustrated / anxious / disappointed / confused / concerned]; intensity (1-10): [...]
Calm enough to talk productively? [yes: proceed / no: wait until (date and time)]

### Impact
On me: [...]   On the team: [...]   On the work: [...]   On our relationship: [...]

### Their constraints and cares (for my preparation only; never state their motive as fact)
- Pressures or limits they may be under: [...]
- A positive intent that could sit behind the behavior: [...]
- What they value that I should acknowledge: [...]

### My part, in more detail
- What I could have done differently: [...]
- What I am willing to own in the conversation: [...]

### Goals
Primary outcome: [...]; secondary: [...]
Best case: [...]; acceptable: [...]; deal breaker: [...]
Relationship goal: [...]; identity goal: [...]

### Questions to ask and proposals
- Questions: [...]
- Proposals I could offer: [...]

### If I get triggered
My triggers here: [...]; my plan to stay calm: [...]

### Logistics
- [ ] Time that works for both, private place booked
- [ ] Enough time allotted ([...] minutes), with a buffer
- [ ] Backup plan if we are interrupted: [...]
- [ ] Follow-up note within one working day; check-in booked for [date]
- [ ] Documentation needed: [yes / no]
```

## Preparation failure modes

Name the one that fits what the user describes, with its fix.

| Failure | Signs | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over-preparing | A word-for-word script, 20 anticipated replies, more anxiety rather than less, delaying to "prepare more" | Prepare themes, not scripts: know the key points and the opening, then trust yourself. |
| Under-preparing | "Winging it", no thought of their view, no specific examples, no clear outcome | At minimum, complete the quick prep above. |
| Preparing to attack | Notes list what they did wrong, a case built like a trial, rehearsed "gotcha" moments, the outcome already decided | Seek understanding, not a win. Add: what might I be missing, and what is their view? |
| Preparing to avoid | An opening full of softeners, a plan to hint rather than state, escape routes, hoping they raise it first | Write a direct opening and say it aloud. |
| Waiting too long | The issue compounds while the user keeps preparing | Fix a dated time (SKILL.md Section 2). |
