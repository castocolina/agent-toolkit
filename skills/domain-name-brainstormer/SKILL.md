# Domain Name Brainstormer

Create a defensible shortlist; do not imply that creative suggestions are registered or legally cleared.

## Procedure

1. **Get constraints.** Ask only for missing decisions that materially change candidates: audience/market, languages, tone, concepts to include/avoid, preferred TLDs, budget including renewals, and pronunciation/spelling constraints.
2. **Create distinct directions.** Extract the product's core concept and useful morphemes. Generate candidates using distinct patterns: compounds (`pixel + garden`), prefix/suffix (`get`, `-kit`, `-wise`), evocative real words, coined blends, and carefully justified abbreviations. Avoid producing superficial spelling variants as if they were different ideas.
3. **Screen aloud and across languages.** For each finalist, assess pronunciation from spelling, likely misspellings, ambiguous word boundaries, unwanted meanings in target languages, and similarity to established brands. Cluster candidates by naming direction and TLD fit.
4. **Rank, don't overclaim.** Default to 10 candidates across `.com` and two fitting alternatives. Explain the rationale and trade-off; keep defensive registrations optional and budget-dependent because renewals recur.
5. **Verify separately on request or when tools are available.** Search a registrar or authoritative registration-data service (RDAP, e.g. `https://rdap.org/domain/example.com`). Record the source and check time. A negative/empty lookup is not legal clearance and may not reflect premium listings or registrar availability. If no live lookup is performed, label every candidate **unverified**.
6. **Clear legal risk separately.** Recommend trademark screening in the relevant jurisdictions and product category before investment. Do not claim legal clearance or treat domain registration as trademark permission.

## Gotchas

- Never claim a domain is available without a live check; registration status changes and registry data can be delayed.
- Never invent prices or estimate a taken domain's value. Distinguish registry status, registrar purchase availability, premium listing, and quoted renewal price.
- Avoid Unicode lookalikes and IDN homographs unless the audience explicitly expects them; visually similar characters can direct users to impersonation sites.
- Check pronunciation and unintended meanings in the actual target languages, not only English; transliteration can change both.
- `.dev` and `.app` require HTTPS in browsers due to HSTS preload. Include deployment implications when suggesting them.
- Do not build a candidate around another company's mark or imply that a similar domain is safe; trademark clearance is separate legal work.

## Output

Group names by creative direction, then give a ranked shortlist with: candidate, intended pronunciation, rationale, spelling/confusion concern, plausible TLD fit, and registration status (`unverified` unless checked). For checked status include lookup source and timestamp. State that prices/renewal terms must be confirmed with the registrar.
