# Reaction Scripts

Responses to rehearse once the opener is drafted (the opener and rules live in `SKILL.md`). Each keeps the conversation on the specific instance instead of following the reaction.

## Reactions that derail the outcome

**Deflection to workload** ("I've been so busy with [other project]...")
> "That's worth discussing. Let's look at your commitments, because I still need [expectation]."

Workload is a real input to the plan, not a reason to drop the expectation.

**Denial of the facts** ("That didn't happen.")
> "Let me make sure we mean the same instance: [date, artifact, what happened]. Does that match what you remember?"

If it still does not match, treat it as disputed (see `SKILL.md`) instead of repeating the claim louder.

**First time hearing it** ("This is the first I'm hearing about this.")
> "That's fair. I should have raised it sooner. Now that it's on the table, can we talk about going forward?"

Owning the delay ends the argument about timing; defending it restarts it.

**Agreement without change** ("You're right, I'll do better.")
> "Thanks. What will you do differently, specifically, and what would help?"

Vague agreement is the most common way a feedback conversation ends with nothing changed.

**Upset, silence, or anger:** describe what is visible ("this seems hard to hear"), never the feeling as fact, and offer a break rather than pushing through.

## Substitutions that prevent non-obvious failures

| Avoid | Why it fails | Use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "People have mentioned that..." | The person cannot answer an unnamed source, and it exposes the reporter | Your own observation, or nothing |
| "You seem checked out." | A feeling stated as fact | The observable instance |
| "Don't take this personally." | Signals the message is personal | Drop it and give the specific instance |

## Conflict between two people (separate, then joint)

Use only when the joint-facilitation conditions in `SKILL.md` hold.

**Separately, each person first:**
> "In [review/date], your comment said [exact wording]. [Observed work impact]. How did you see that exchange?"

With no instance of your own, ask a claim-free information-gathering question ("How is working on [topic] going for you?"); that is not SBI feedback and does not replace it.

**Joint session:**
> "My goal is a way forward on [topic], not a verdict on who was right. [Person A], please share your view; [Person B], please hold your response until A finishes, then it's your turn."

**Common ground:**
> "You both want [shared goal]. Where you differ is [specific disagreement]. Is that right?"

**Close with a process, not just a truce:**
> "How should we decide the next time this kind of disagreement comes up?"

Then follow up per `SKILL.md`.
