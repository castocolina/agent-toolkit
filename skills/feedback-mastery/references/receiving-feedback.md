# Receiving Feedback

For when the feedback the user received names specific instances and they must reply, confirm it back, or follow up. Vague labels ("you're unreliable") are handled in `SKILL.md` section 4; once an instance is on the table, use this file.

## During the conversation

1. **Listen to the end.** Do not draft a rebuttal while they talk.
2. **Ask for what is missing:** "Can you give me a specific example?" One instance and its effect are enough.
3. **Paraphrase to confirm:** "So what I'm hearing is [the instance] and [its effect]. Did I get that right?"
4. **Acknowledge the effect, not their reading of your motive:** "I can see that cost you a day." You can do this even if your intent was different; it concedes the effect, not the intent. If the intent matters, say it once, briefly, after the acknowledgment.
5. **Thank them and do not defend in the meeting.** For a claim you dispute, name what would settle it (ticket history, a timeline, the reviewer) and look at it afterwards, as in `SKILL.md` section 4.

## After the conversation

1. **Reflect:** is there truth in the instance they described, and what part was yours?
2. **Pick one or two actions** you will change, concrete enough to check ("run the suite before opening a PR").
3. **Tell them what you are changing**, within a few days, with the date it starts. The written recap in `SKILL.md` section 6 is the vehicle, and the recipient of feedback may send it.
4. **Ask for ongoing feedback** in a form they can use: "If you see it again, flag it the same day."
